# Decisions

Some decisions I have made on filing rules based on transactions I had uncertainty about.

## Is a re-occuring payment a subscription or not?

Basic guidline is that if I can cancel it or find a cheaper deal in a short period of time, I will consider it as a subscription.

Take for example rent vs. my home internet plan. I can switch or straight up not use internet if shit hits the fan, but I'm locked into my rent for however long my lease is signed till. By this example rent + electricity payments are not subscriptions, most other things like mobile plans are.

## Purchase Dates

In general, for transaction date, use the purchase date and not the posted date, only use posted date if I forget when I bought the thing. Not important rule who cares about a drift of few days.

## Merchant Name

The merchant is whoever I would have paid if I had paid directly, not whoever actually fronted the money. So when a friend covers the restaurant bill and I send him my share on Venmo, the merchant is the restaurant. When a person registers our intramural team and I pay him my share, the merchant is BU Intramurals.

The exception is when the person really is the merchant, like buying a friend's old monitor. There is no third party behind him then, so his name is correct.

## Payment Method

If it can hold a balance it counts as the payment method, if it is only a rail then use the underlying card. Venmo and PayPal hold balances so they get recorded as themselves. Apple Pay and Google Pay hold nothing, so those would be whichever card they charged.

## Friends Paying Me Back

When I front the bill and people send me their share, record the full amount I paid and add a reimbursement with source Family, rather than only entering my own share.

Both end up with the same net, but only this way shows how much I am fronting for people.
